4  Handle data gaps explicitly

NoteLearning objective

You understand why a safety-critical library by default raises an exception on missing values instead of silently interpolating, and you can correctly apply the three modes raise, ffill_bfill, passthrough of the class LinearlyInterpolateTS.

ImportantPreferred strategy: annotation

In safety-critical pipelines the annotation strategy from Chapter 3 (mark_outliers + get_missing_weights + WeightFunction) is the preferred one, because it does not replace gaps with invented values — broken rows are consistently excluded from the loss with weight 0. The linear interpolation shown here remains relevant for three situations: (1) migration from legacy notebooks, (2) as a follow-up step after a deliberately non-imputing imputer, and (3) when the downstream processing demands a NaN-free series without an explicit weight series.

4.1 Why are data gaps an AI Act topic?

Real sensor data contain gaps. A weather station fails for an hour, an electricity meter is rebooted, a data export drops an empty field. In a pure research notebook this is harmless. In a high-risk application under Annex III of the AI Act — such as the electric load forecast that a transmission system operator uses for dispatch — an unnoticed gap is dangerous. It produces a forecast that is returned with the same apparent confidence as a forecast without a gap; the auditor will never notice afterwards.

Code rule CR-3 (fail-safe processing) therefore requires: invalid inputs explicitly raise an exception instead of being silently imputed. The AI Act underpins this discipline in Art. 10(3) (representativeness, error-freedom, completeness) and Art. 15 (accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity).

pandas, scikit-learn, skforecast, Darts, statsmodels — most tools in the Python ecosystem follow the “silently keep computing” pattern. spotforecast2-safe inverts this default.

4.2 The three modes of LinearlyInterpolateTS

Since the major version with the contract switch on_missing, LinearlyInterpolateTS makes the treatment of remaining NaN values explicit. The class interpolates inside the series linearly (limit_area="inside"), so middle gaps are cleanly closed, while endpoint gaps remain and are handled further according to the mode:

Mode Behaviour Who uses this?
"raise" (default) Raises ValueError with a preview of the first gaps as soon as any NaN values remain after interpolation. Safety-critical pipeline; declares CR-3 actively.
"ffill_bfill" Fills the remaining endpoint gaps by forward/backward fill. Migration from legacy notebooks; internally also part of the forecaster’s training contract.
"passthrough" Passes NaN through unchanged; the next step takes responsibility. When a downstream imputer (e.g. WeightedAverageImputation) handles the gaps deliberately.

4.4 Endpoint gaps: here CR-3 shows its effect

y_edge = y.copy()
y_edge.iloc[0:2] = np.nan        # the first two hours are missing

try:
    LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="raise").fit_transform(y_edge)
except ValueError as exc:
    print("Expected fail-safe abort:")
    print(str(exc))
Expected fail-safe abort:
2 missing value(s) remain after linear interpolation. First gaps: [2024-01-01 00:00:00+00:00, 2024-01-01 01:00:00+00:00]. Pass on_missing='ffill_bfill' to opt into endpoint forward/backward fill or on_missing='passthrough' to return the interpolated series with residual NaN.

The error message contains a preview of the first gaps — that is all a dispatcher or auditor needs to locate the gap precisely. Exactly this visibility is what Art. 12 of the AI Act (record-keeping) demands: nothing must fall under the table.

4.5 ffill_bfill as a deliberate opt-in

If you want to tolerate endpoint gaps deliberately (e.g. at the start of a migration), you must declare this explicitly:

y_filled = LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="ffill_bfill").fit_transform(y_edge)
print(f"NaN after 'ffill_bfill':   {int(y_filled.isna().sum())}")
print("First five hours:", y_filled.iloc[:5].round(2).tolist())
NaN after 'ffill_bfill':   0
First five hours: [37123.23, 37123.23, 37123.23, 36753.52, 37051.4]

Internally ForecasterRecursiveModel uses exactly this configuration in eight places — not out of carelessness, but to satisfy the training invariant “no NaN in the lag window at training time” even when the caller passes the series without pre-processing. The contract is made visible at the call site; that is the difference from silent imputation.

4.6 passthrough: responsibility passed to the caller

y_pt = LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="passthrough").fit_transform(y_edge)
print(f"NaN after 'passthrough':  {int(y_pt.isna().sum())}")
NaN after 'passthrough':  2

Useful, for example, when a downstream WeightedAverageImputation step fills the gaps deliberately. The WARNING emitted by that imputer is shown so that the caller sees in the audit log that imputation has happened.

4.8 Which strategy when? — a comparison at a glance

The three LinearlyInterpolateTS modes and the annotation pipeline from Chapter 3 are not mutually exclusive — they target different points in the lifecycle of the data. Before mapping the application situations, a direct comparison of the two strategies’ structural properties is worthwhile:

Property LinearlyInterpolateTS mark_outliers + get_missing_weights (Chapter 3)
Series length after pre-processing unchanged unchanged
NaN guarantee after the step only in ffill_bfill mode / after interpolation yes, guaranteed after get_missing_weights
Training target contains invented values yes (line between neighbours) no (real values; broken values are ffill placeholders with weight 0)
Auditability of the action indirect (raise error message) direct (verbose=True lists indices; weights is serialisable)
Model bias due to imputation possible structurally excluded (weight 0)
Recommendation for high-risk applications only as a migration path or with a downstream imputer standard strategy

The following table translates these structural differences into concrete application situations:

Situation Recommended strategy
Production high-risk pipeline (Annex III) Annotation (Chapter 3); interpolation only as a downstream special step
Middle gaps of individual hours, no systematic outlier search needed LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="raise") as a CR-3 switch
Migration from legacy notebooks without a weight_func path LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="ffill_bfill") as a bridge
Own downstream imputer LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="passthrough")
Audit requires an explicit, picklable weight series Annotation (Chapter 3); weights are part of the model artefact

If in doubt, start with the annotation from Chapter 3 and only fall back to LinearlyInterpolateTS when there is a concrete argument against the weight series.

## Which strategy when? --- a comparison at a glance

The three `LinearlyInterpolateTS` modes and the annotation pipeline from
@sec-annotation are not mutually exclusive --- they target different
points in the lifecycle of the data. Before mapping the application
situations, a direct comparison of the two strategies' *structural
properties* is worthwhile:

| Property                                  | `LinearlyInterpolateTS`                              | `mark_outliers` + `get_missing_weights` (@sec-annotation) |
|:------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------|
| Series length after pre-processing        | unchanged                                          | unchanged                                                |
| NaN guarantee after the step              | only in `ffill_bfill` mode / after interpolation    | yes, guaranteed after `get_missing_weights`              |
| Training target contains invented values  | yes (line between neighbours)                       | no (real values; broken values are ffill placeholders with weight 0) |
| Auditability of the action                | indirect (`raise` error message)                    | direct (`verbose=True` lists indices; `weights` is serialisable) |
| Model bias due to imputation              | possible                                           | structurally excluded (weight 0)                          |
| Recommendation for high-risk applications | only as a migration path or with a downstream imputer | **standard strategy**                                  |

The following table translates these structural differences into
concrete application situations:

| Situation                                                    | Recommended strategy                                  |
|:-------------------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------|
| Production high-risk pipeline (Annex III)                    | **Annotation** (@sec-annotation); interpolation only as a downstream special step |
| Middle gaps of individual hours, no systematic outlier search needed | `LinearlyInterpolateTS(on_missing="raise")` as a CR-3 switch |
